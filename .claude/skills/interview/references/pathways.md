# Pathways

How to turn her intake answers into 2–3 suggested pathways with honest fit reasoning.

## Pick pathways in this order

1. **Filter by her constraints first (Q6, Q10–Q12).** Schedule, start date, hours, pay, and location. Her #1 ranked item (Q13) never breaks. Drop any pathway that can't meet it or her other stated limits. Never suggest full-time to someone who said school hours.
2. **Match her interests (Q7–Q8)** using the "Fits" column below.
3. **Use what she already has (Q1, Q3, Q5).** Her last job, credentials (including lapsed ones), and checklist skills. A lapsed license that can be renewed is often the fastest route.
4. **Rank by her top 3 (Q13).** Put first the pathways that best meet her #1, then #2, then #3.
5. **Add the wildcard (Q9)** as a third option if it is realistic, and say plainly what it would take.
6. **Run the reality check** (below) on every pathway before you show it.

If nothing fits all her constraints, flex in this order: items she did not rank, then her #3, then her #2. Say which constraint is the tightest and ask if it can flex.

Offer **2–3**, never more. For each, write 3–4 short lines:
- **Why it fits:** quote her answers ("you liked working with people, and you ran the PTA volunteer schedule").
- **What it needs:** training or credential, in plain words.
- **How it fits her life:** schedule and flexibility.
- **First step:** one concrete action, with a resource from `utah-resources.md` when one fits.

## Numbers: pay, cost, and training time

Never state a pay range, tuition cost, or exact training length from memory.
- Name sources in full words. Write "the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS)" the first time, never a bare acronym.
- **With web search:** look it up for Utah, cite the source, and tag it `[UNVERIFIED — web search]`. Prefer government data (BLS, jobs.utah.gov) over job-board estimates.
- **Without web search:** say "I don't have a verified number for that — DWS career coaches can give you current Utah figures."
- If she said pay is "not sure," point out that the long-term view matters too (raises, retirement, Social Security credits), not only this year's childcare cost.

## Reality check

Before she sees any pathway, prove it is a real job that can meet her constraints. A pathway that sounds right but has no real postings wastes her time and trust.

**Use a separate checker agent when one is available.** Give it only:
- the pathway names you plan to show,
- her constraints and ranking (Q6, Q10–Q13),
- the instruction below.

Do not give it the conversation or your reasoning, so it judges the pathways fresh and does not just agree with you.

Checker instruction: *"For each pathway, try to prove it is NOT a real job path for this person. Look for (1) a matching occupation in the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data or O*NET, and (2) at least two current job postings in Utah, or remote ones open to Utah residents, that match her hours and location. Return for each: real / not proven / fails her constraints, with the links you found."*

**Without an agent:** run the same check yourself with web search.

**Without web search:** keep only pathways from the table above, and say "I couldn't check current postings — DWS career coaches can confirm what's hiring now."

**Act on the result:**
- **Real:** show it.
- **Not proven:** show it only with a plain note, e.g., "I couldn't find current Utah postings for this one."
- **Fails her constraints:** drop it, or show it only as the wildcard with the gap said plainly.

Tag all web findings `[UNVERIFIED — web search]`.

## Tone

- Be honest about trade-offs: "This one starts lower but grows fastest."
- Never imply a pathway is guaranteed. Use "a realistic option" rather than "you'd be perfect for."
