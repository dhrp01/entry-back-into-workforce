# Ideal Customer Profile & Primary Persona

Who this app is built for. Every design, copy, and feature decision should be tested against this profile: *"Would this work for Melissa, on her phone, at 9:40pm, after the kids are down?"*

---

## ICP Summary

**A Utah mother who has been home as the primary caregiver for 12+ months and wants (or needs) to return to paid work — but doesn't know where to start.**

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Utah — Wasatch Front core (Salt Lake, Utah, Davis, Weber counties), with rural users in Phase 2 |
| **Age** | 28–50 (center: mid-30s) |
| **Household** | Married or single parent; 2–4 kids, at least one not yet in full-day school is common |
| **Career break** | 1–15 years (center: 4–8 years); left a real career — teaching, healthcare, admin, retail management, marketing, accounting — to raise children |
| **Education** | Some college through bachelor's degree; certifications often lapsed |
| **Tech comfort** | Smartphone-first; comfortable with Instagram/Pinterest/Venmo; intimidated by LinkedIn and ATS portals |
| **Work goal** | Often part-time or flexible/remote first, school-hours friendly; full-time if pay justifies childcare |
| **Income context** | Household feels the squeeze (Utah cost of living, housing); her income is needed or strongly wanted, but childcare math makes the decision genuinely hard |

**Why Utah-specific matters:** Utah has the nation's largest average household sizes and a strong stay-at-home-parent culture; women's workforce participation dips most in the 25–54 prime-career years. Many ICP users carry significant unpaid leadership experience through church callings, PTA, and community volunteering — exactly the skills the app translates. Utah also leads the nation in women working part-time, which matches the flexible-first re-entry goal.

## Primary Persona: "Melissa"

**Melissa Hansen, 38 — Lehi, Utah**

- Married, 4 kids (15, 12, 8, 5). Youngest just started kindergarten — *the trigger.*
- Was an elementary school teacher for 5 years before leaving when kid #2 arrived. Teaching license lapsed 8 years ago.
- Since then: PTA vice president, Primary teacher at church, ran the neighborhood youth soccer league schedule, managed a kitchen renovation, keeps the family budget in a spreadsheet she's quietly proud of.
- Household income is okay but tight — a car repair is a stressful event. Her working would change the family's margin and fund college savings.
- Has thought about going back for two years. Opened LinkedIn twice. Closed it twice.

### What she says (voice of customer)
- "I've been out so long, who would hire me?"
- "I don't even know what I'd *do*. I don't want to teach again, but it's all I've done."
- "By the time I pay for after-school care for two kids, what's the point?"
- "Everyone my age has had a career this whole time. I'm starting from zero."
- "I can't take a job that falls apart every time a kid gets sick."

### Goals
1. A job that fits school hours or is flexible/remote
2. To feel *competent and current* again — identity, not just income
3. A clear answer to "what should I do and what's my first step?"
4. To re-enter without starting completely at the bottom

### Pain points (mapped to app features)
| Pain | App answer |
|---|---|
| "I have no skills" | Activity checklist → watch skill profile build in real time |
| "I don't know what I'd do" | Aptitude questions → 2–3 pathways with honest fit reasoning |
| "My resume is a crater" | Gap-owning resume builder + practiced gap story |
| "Childcare math doesn't work" | Money-math calculator (incl. long-term view) + childcare assistance / Kids in Care connections |
| "I don't know anyone anymore" | Peer cohort + mentor match + warm handoffs (Return Utah, Tech-Moms) |
| "I only have scraps of time" | 10-minute modules, progress always saved |
| "I'll probably give up when it gets hard" | Rejection-recovery content, check-in nudges, celebration mechanics |

### Triggers (when she downloads the app)
- Youngest starts school
- Budget shock (layoff scare, rising costs, medical bill)
- Divorce or loss (see Segment C)
- A friend successfully returns to work
- New year / school year ("this is my year")

### Objections to overcome
- "This will tell me to 'just network' like every article I've read" → *No: it ends with an actual plan.*
- "Assessments make me feel dumb" → *No wrong answers; skills appear, not scores.*
- "It'll pressure me into full-time" → *Constraints come first; pathways respect them.*
- "Is my information safe?" → *Plain-language privacy promise; minimal data stored.*

### Where to reach her (channels)
- Instagram and Facebook mom groups (Utah-specific groups are huge and active)
- School newsletters, PTA networks
- Church community networks (handled respectfully, never presumptively)
- Local libraries and rec centers
- Partners: Return Utah, Tech-Moms, DWS employment centers, Utah Women & Leadership Project events

### Success looks like (for Melissa)
Within 60 days: a skill profile she believes, a resume she's not ashamed of, one chosen pathway, two community connections, and three applications submitted. Within 6 months: hired into a role that fits her life — or enrolled in the certificate program that gets her there.

## Secondary Segments (same core journey, different tuning)

| Segment | Difference from Melissa | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Short-gap returner** (12–24 months) | Skills fresher, confidence dip smaller, often needs speed over exploration | Fast-track mode: lighter assessment, straight to resume + job board |
| **B. Long-gap / empty-nester** (10+ years, kids grown) | Deeper tech-currency gap, bigger identity rebuild, more hours available | Heavier skill-builder emphasis; digital-skills refreshers; full-time pathways |
| **C. Necessity returner** (divorce, widowhood, partner job loss) | Urgent income need, grief/stress load, possible benefits navigation | Tone must not be "exciting new chapter!"; fastest-income pathways; benefits-cliff tool; 211/legal/childcare resources surfaced early |
| **D. Never-worked or minimal work history** | No pre-gap career to draw on | Checklist and volunteer translation carry the whole profile; entry pathways with built-in training (apprenticeships, certificates) |

## Anti-ICP (who this app is *not* optimized for — v1)

- Executives seeking senior-level re-entry (iRelaunch and private returnships serve them)
- Students/new grads with no caregiving gap
- Users needing intensive case management beyond app + warm handoff (refer to DWS/211 directly)

Keeping v1 focused on Melissa is what makes the app feel like it was built *for* someone rather than for everyone.
